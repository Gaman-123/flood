1. Introduction  
1.1 Purpose   
The purpose of this Software Requirements Specification (SRS) document  is to provide a 
comprehensive and detailed description of the Library Management System (LMS), version 1.0. This 
document aims to clearly define the functional and non -functional requirements of the system, 
ensuring a shared understanding among all stakeholders involved in the development process. 
The scope of this SRS includes the com plete functionality of the LMS, covering modules such as 
book inventory management, user account handling, borrowing and return processes, and reporting 
mechanisms. The docum ent serves as a formal agreement between stakeholders , including clients, 
developers, and project managers, regarding what the system is expected to accomplish. 
Additionally, this SRS will act as a reference point throughout the software development lifecy cle, 
supporting activities such as system design, implementation, testing, and maintenance. It is intended 
to minimize ambiguity, reduce development risks, and ensure that the final system aligns with user 
expectations and organizational objectives. 
1.2 Document Conventions  
This document follows standard IEEE documentation conventions to ensure clarity, consistency, and 
readability. The structure is organized into hierarchical sections and subsections, each identified by 
numbered headings. 
The following conventions are used throughout the document: 
• Bold text is used for section headings and important terms to improve readability.  
• The term “shall” is used to indicate mandatory system requirements that must be fulfilled.  
• The term “should” is used to indicate recommended requirements that are desirable but not 
strictly mandatory.  
• The term “may” is used to indicate optional features or functionalities.  
Each functional requirement is uniquely ide ntified using a standardized labeling format (e.g., REQ -
LMS-01, REQ-LMS-02) to ensure traceability and ease of reference during development and testing 
phases. 
Priority levels are assigned to requirements as follows: 
• High (H): Critical for system operation  
• Medium (M): Important but not critical  
• Low (L): Optional or enhancement features

1.4 Product Scope  
The Library Management System ( LMS) is a software solution designed to automate and optimize 
the core operations of a library. The system aims to replace traditional manual processes  with an 
efficient, reliable, and user-friendly digital platform. 
The primary objective of the LMS is to streamline activities such as book cataloging, user registration, 
borrowing and returning of books, and fine calculation. By centralizing all library-related data into a 
unified system, the LMS enhances accuracy, reduces redundancy, and improves overall op erational 
efficiency. 
Key Goals: 
• To provide a centralized database for managing books and users  
• To enable quick and efficient searching of library resources  
 
3 
 
• To automate transaction processes such as issuing and returning books  
• To minimize human errors associated with manual record keeping  
Expected Benefits: 
• Improved efficiency and time savings  
• Enhanced user experience through intuitive interfaces  
• Better tracking and management of library assets  
• Increased reliability and data consistency  
The LMS aligns with organizational goals of digital transformation and improved service delivery, 
making it a valuable asset for modern educational institutions.

2. Requirements Gathering & Analysis 
2.1 Customer Interaction Overview 
The requirements for the Library Managemen t System (LMS) were elicited through structured and 
simulated interactions with key stakeholders representing the primary user groups of the system. 
Since this is an academic exercise, these interactions were modeled to reflect real -world scenarios 
typically encountered in library environments. 
The stakeholders involved in the requirement gathering process include: 
• Librarians, who are responsible for managing daily library operations  
• Students, who are the primary users of library services  
• Faculty members, who utilize the library for academic and research purposes  
• System administrators, who oversee system maintenance and control  
 
4 
 
Each stakeholder group contributes unique requirements based on their roles and expectations. For 
instance, librarians emphasized the need for efficient book tracking and inventory management, while 
students highlighted the importance of quick search and easy borrowing processes. 
The interaction process ensured that both functional and non -functional requirements were captured 
comprehensively. It also helped in identifying potential challenges and usability concer ns that could 
arise during system implementation. 
2.2 Interview Method 
A structured interview methodology was adopted to gather detailed and consistent information from 
stakeholders. A predefined set of questions was prepared to guide the interviews and en sure that all 
relevant aspects of the system were addressed. 
Objectives of the Interview Process: 
• To understand the limitations of existing systems  
• To identify user expectations and requirements  
• To gather insights into daily operational challenges  
• To define system functionalities from a user-centric perspective  
Sample Interview Questions: 
• What difficulties do you face while managing or using the current library system?  
• How do you currently track issued and returned books?  
• What features would you like to see in a new system?  
• How should overdue books and fines be handled?  
Summary of Responses: 
• Librarians expressed the need for automation to reduce manual workload and errors  
• Students emphasized the importance of fast and accurate search functionality  
• Faculty members required extended borrowing privileges and better tracking  
• Administrators highlighted the need for reporting and monitoring capabilities  
The responses were carefully analyzed and categorized into functional requirements (system 
behavior) and non-functional requirements (performance, usability, security, etc.). 
2.3 Brainstorming Session 
In addition to interviews, brainstorming sessions were conducted among the development team to 
generate ideas and refine system requirements. This collaborative approach encouraged creative 
thinking and helped identify additional features that could enhance the system. 
 
5 
 
During the brainstorming sessions: 
• Team members proposed various features and improvements  
• Ideas were evaluated based on feasibility, cost, and impact  
• Redundant or impractical suggestions were filtered out  
Key Outcomes: 
• Inclusion of advanced search filters (by category, author, availability)  
• Implementation of automated fine calculation  
• Development of a user-friendly interface  
• Addition of reporting and analytics features  
The brainstorming process played a crucial role in expanding the scope of the system while 
ensuring that it remains practical and implementable. 
2.4 Observation Study 
An observation study was conducted to analy ze the workflow of existing library systems. Both 
manual systems (register-based) and semi-automated systems (spreadsheet-based) were examined. 
Observed Processes: 
• Manual entry of book records in registers  
• Recording of issue and return transactions  
• Calculation of fines using basic methods  
Key Findings: 
• Manual systems are time-consuming and prone to human errors  
• Data retrieval is inefficient and often delayed  
• Lack of centralized data storage leads to inconsistencies  
• Tracking overdue books is difficult and unreliable  
These observations highlighted the need for a centralized, automated system that can handle large 
volumes of data efficiently and accurately. 
2.5 Business Problem Description 
The existing library management approach suffers from several critical issues that negatively 
impact efficiency and user satisfaction. 
 
6 
 
Identified Problems: 
• Dependence on manual processes for record keeping  
• Difficulty in maintaining accurate and up-to-date records  
• Inefficient search and retrieval of book information  
• Lack of real-time tracking of issued and returned books  
• Errors in fine calculation and reporting  
These problems lead to increased workload fo r library staff, delays in service delivery , and 
dissatisfaction among users. The absence of a centralized system further exacerbates these issues by 
causing data inconsistencies and duplication. 
The proposed Library Management System aims to address these  challenges by providing an 
integrated, automated solution that improves efficiency, accuracy, and user experience.

manual library systems. It serves as a centralized platform for managing all library-related activities,

• Student Information Systems (SIS)  
• Enterprise Resource Planning (ERP) systems

The LMS interacts with external entities such as users (students, faculty) and administrators, forming 
a complete ecosystem for library management.

The LMS provides a wide range of fu nctions that collectively support efficient library operations.

• Fine Management: Calculate and record fines for overdue books  
• Search Functionality: Locate books using various search criteria

These functions are interconnected and work together to provide a seamless user experience.

6. Other Nonfunctional Requirements  
Non-functional requirements define the quality attributes and constraints under which the Library 
Management System (LMS) must operate . These requirements do not describe specific system 
behaviors but instead specify crit eria that judge the system’s performance, reliability, security, and 
usability. 
6.1 Performance Requirements  
The performance requirements specify how efficiently the system should operate under various 
conditions. These requirements ensure that the LMS pr ovides a smooth and responsive user 
experience even during peak usage. 
Response Time 
• The system shall respond to user actions (such as search queries o r login requests) within 2 
seconds under normal operating conditions.  
• For complex operations such as rep ort generation, the response time shall not exceed 5 
seconds.  
Concurrency 
• The system shall support a minimum of 100 concurrent users without significant degradation 
in performance.  
• The system shall maintain consistent performance levels during peak usage hours.  
Throughput 
• The system shall be capable of processing multiple transactions (such as issuing or returning 
books) simultaneously.  
• Database operations shall be optimized to ensure minimal delay in data retrieval and updates.  
Scalability 
• The system shall be scalable to accommodate an increasing number of users and records 
without requiring major architectural changes.  
 
20 
 
Rationale 
These requirements ensure that the system remains efficient and responsive, thereby improving user 
satisfaction and operational productivity. 
6.2 Safety Requirements  
Safety requirements focus on preventing data loss, system failures, and unintended actions that could 
harm the system or its users. 
Data Protection 
• The system shall implement regular data backup mechanisms to pre vent loss of critical 
information.  
• Backup operations shall be performed automatically at scheduled intervals.  
Error Handling 
• The system shall detect and handle errors gracefully without crashing.  
• In case of system failure, appropriate error messages shall be displayed to the user.  
Data Integrity 
• The system shall ensure that all transactions are completed accurately and consistently.  
• Partial or incomplete transactions shall be rolled back to maintain system consistency.  
Preventive Measures 
• The system s hall prevent accidental deletion of important records by requiring user 
confirmation.  
• Critical operations shall be restricted to authorized users only.  
Rationale 
These safety measures ensure the reliability of the system and protect against potential dat a loss or 
corruption. 
6.3 Security Requirements  
Security requirements define how the system protects user data and prevents unauthorized access. 
Authentication 
• The system shall require users to log in using valid credentials (username and password).  
 
21 
 
• Passwords shall be stored in encrypted form.  
Authorization 
• The system shall implement role-based access control (RBAC).  
• Different user roles (admin, librarian, student) shall have different access privileges.  
Data Security 
• Sensitive data shall be encrypted during transmission using secure protocols (e.g., HTTPS).  
• The system shall prevent unauthorized access to confidential information.  
Session Management 
• The system shall automatically log out users after a period of inactivity.  
• The system shall prevent session hijacking through secure session handling.  
Audit and Monitoring 
• The system shall maintain logs of user activities for monitoring and auditing purposes.  
• Unauthorized access attempts shall be recorded and flagged.  
Rationale 
Security requirements are critical to protect sensitive user data and maintain trust in the system.

• Only administrators shall have permission to modify system configurations.  
Book Management Rules 
• Books must be uniquely identified using ISBN or system-generated IDs.  
• Duplicate entries shall not be allowed.  
Operational Rules 
• Users must have valid accounts to access system features.  
• Inactive accounts may be deactivated after a specified period.  
Rationale 
These rules ensure consistent system behavior and align the software with organizational policies and 
procedures. 
7. Other Requirements  
This section specifies additional requirements that are not covered in earlier sections of the Software 
Requirements Specification. These requirements address supporting aspects such as database design, 
legal considerations, scalability, and future enhancements. 
 
7.1 Database Requirements 
The Library Management System shall use a structured relational database to store and manage all  
system data efficiently. 
Database Characteristics 
• The system shall use a Relational Database Management System (RDBMS) such as MySQL.  
• All data shall be stored in normalized tables to reduce redundancy and improve consistency.  
• The database shall maintain  relationships between entities such as books, user s, and 
transactions.  
Data Entities 
The system shall include the following primary entities: 
 
24 
 
• Books (Book ID, Title, Author, ISBN, Category, Availability)  
• Users (User ID, Name, Role, Contact Details)  
• Transactions (Transaction ID, Issue Date, Return Date, Fine Amount)  
Data Integrity 
• The system shall enforce referential integrity constraints.  
• Primary and foreign keys shall be used to maintain relationships.  
7.2 Legal and Regulatory Requirements 
The LMS m ust comply with applicable legal and institutional policies regarding data usage and 
privacy. 
• User data shall be handled in accordance with data protection regulations.  
• The system shall ensure that personal information is not disclosed to unauthorized parties.  
• Copyright rules shall be respected when managing digital resources. 
7.3 Internationalization and Localization Requirements 
Although the initial version of the system is designed for a specific region, it should support future 
expansion. 
• The system shall support multiple languages in future versions.  
• Date and time formats shall be adaptable based on regional settings.  
• Currency formats (for fines) shall be configurable. 
7.4 Scalability and Future Enhancements 
The system shall be designed to accommodate future growth and feature expansion. 
• The system architecture shall support the addition of new modules.  
• Future enhancements may include mobile application support.  
• Integration with external systems (e.g., digital libraries) may be implemented. 
 
7.5 Data Backup and Recovery 
• The system shall perform periodic backups of all critical data.  
• Backup data shall be stored securely and be recoverable in case of system failure.  
• Recovery procedures shall ensure minimal downtime. 
 
25 
 
7.6 Reusability Requirements 
• The system shall be developed using modular components to allow reuse in other projects.  
• Code components such as authentication modules may be reused in future systems.
